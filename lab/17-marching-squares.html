Field Notes — extraction of iso-lines

An implicit surface is a shape stored as an equation rather than as geometry. We evaluate a scalar field f(x, y, t) across a survey grid and recover its outline with our own marching-squares extractor: every grid cell is classified into one of 16 cases from the signs of its four corners, and the contour crosses each bent edge at the linearly interpolated level — genuine sub-cell precision, none of the blocky threshold look.

1 · Implicit fields

metaballs f(p) = Σᵢ rᵢ² / (|p − cᵢ|² + ε) f = 0.5 · (1 + sin(2.6θ + k·r − ωt)) · e^(−2.6r²) (vortex arms) f = fbm(x, y, t) — 4-octave value noise, trilinear + quintic fade

Inside the iso-level T the field reads as land; the boundary of that region is the curve inked. A pointer drag injects one Gaussian bump into the field. The contours re-route around it.

2 · Cell classification

case = (f₀₀ ≥ T) | (f₁₀ ≥ T)«1 | (f₁₁ ≥ T)«2 | (f₀₁ ≥ T)«3 → 16 cases

Each case yields 0–2 contour segments per cell. The two saddle cases (5 and 10) are ambiguous; we settle them with the cell-centre average:

f_c = ¼(f₀₀ + f₁₀ + f₁₁ + f₀₁) → connect or split the saddle

3 · Sub-cell interpolation

t = (T − fₐ) / (f_b − fₐ) crossing point = lerp(cornerₐ, corner_b, t)

The same crossings, joined around the inside corners, close into filled polygons — so one extraction pass serves both the hairline contour and the translucent area wash.

The extractor's output is then dressed as a gilded relief: each filled cell shades itself by banded field height — ten champagne gradients, dark onyx in the valleys, polished gold on the peaks, a specular pass on the highest band. The plate itself carries a camera: pitch and yaw with inertia, idle auto-return. The math is untouched; only the light is new.

4 · Animated iso-level

T(t) = T₀ + A·sin(0.5 t), A = 0.18

Sweeping the level reads out the field's topology: islands merge, lakes open, contours re-route. Level-set reasoning, one rule pass per frame.

Station Notes — construction record

One self-contained HTML file. No frameworks, no CDN, no images, no webfonts. A 2D-canvas renderer rules every mark. Holds 60 fps on a phone.

Engine

  • Field sampler — Float32 grid (40²–180²), three generators: analytic metaballs, value-noise fbm, analytic vortex. Aspect-corrected world coordinates so shapes stay round.
  • Extractor — one pass builds a Path2D for filled regions and a Path2D for contour segments; saddle cells resolved by centre average. Inked as a wide faint gold pass plus a hairline champagne core.
  • Loop — requestAnimationFrame with clamped dt; FPS is an exponential moving average logged in the readout stack.
  • Reduced motion — with prefers-reduced-motion, time stands still: a crisp static frame, every control re-renders on demand.

Interactions

  • Camera — the plate sits on a pitch/yaw head. Drag to turn, release with pace and it coasts; a spring rights it when idle. Amplitude clamped so the survey never reads upside down.
  • Drag the sheet — injects a Gaussian bump at the pointer; the contours chase the cursor.
  • Space halt · R re-survey · N re-seed · 1/2/3 field · F area wash · A sweep level.
  • Faders: iso level, animation speed, grid resolution (re-sampled in real time).
  • Plates P·1–P·4: Lava Lamp, Topography, Vortex Storm, Wireframe.
  • State is shareable — the URL hash encodes field, faders, toggles and seed; reload restores it.

Cost of ink

25k cells × 4 corner tests per frame is trivial for modern JS; the expense sits in stroking thousands of segments, so everything is batched into two Path2D strokes. Device pixel ratio is capped at 2 to keep mobile fill-rate honest.
