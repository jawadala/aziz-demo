One Pair Potential, Many Phases

Melting, crystallisation, pressure, evaporation: all of it follows from one loop. Compute pairwise forces, integrate positions, repeat. Newton's equations, hundreds of spheres, one interaction law: strong repulsion when squeezed, weak attraction when close.

1 · THE PAIR INTERACTION

U(r) = 4ε [ (σ/r)^12 − (σ/r)^6 ] r = centre-to-centre distance σ = sphere diameter (our length unit) ε = attraction depth (our energy unit)

The (σ/r)^12 term is a hard repulsive wall: no overlap. The −(σ/r)^6 term is a van der Waals-type attraction. Differentiate U for the force used by the integrator:

F(r) = −dU/dr = (24ε / r) [ 2(σ/r)^12 − (σ/r)^6 ] F > 0 pushes apart (r < 2^(1/6)σ) · F < 0 pulls together (r > that)

Soft-sphere mode removes the attractive term: pure (σ/r)^12 repulsion. No condensation. Use it as a control against LJ.

2 · STEPPING TIME — VELOCITY VERLET

Symplectic integrator: energy error oscillates instead of drifting, so the fluid runs millions of steps without blowing up:

v(t+½dt) = v(t) + ½·a(t)·dt (half kick) x(t+dt) = x(t) + v(t+½dt)·dt (drift) a(t+dt) = F(x(t+dt)) / m (new forces) v(t+dt) = v(t+½dt) + ½·a(t+dt)·dt (second half kick)

3 · THE CELL LIST — FINDING NEIGHBOURS IN O(N)

All-pairs costs O(N²): 2,000 spheres = ~2M checks per step. Instead, tile the box into cells one cutoff wide. A sphere interacts only with neighbours in its 3×3 cell block:

cutoff rc = 2.5σ (U beyond rc ≈ 0.016ε — negligible) grid cell size = rc → each sphere inspects ≤ a handful of others total work per step: O(N) ← this is the whole trick

4 · TEMPERATURE & THE THERMOSTAT

Temperature = mean kinetic energy per sphere (2D, kB = m = 1):

T* = (1/N) Σ ½ v² (2 degrees of freedom, m = kB = 1) rescale: v ← λ·v , λ = √( 1 + (dt/τ)(T₀/T* − 1) ) T₀ = your slider target · τ = coupling time (gentle, not jarring)

T* = 0: the crystal anneals into place. T* high: the lattice sublimates. The target-vs-measured readout gap shows the thermostat coupling.

5 · WHAT TO TRY

  • Melting: LATTICE at T* = 0.1, raise to 1.5. Hexagonal order dissolves.
  • Freezing: 400 spheres, T* = 0.05. Hexagonal domains grow.
  • Evaporation: few spheres, LJ, low density. Droplets form.
  • Control: same scene, soft-sphere mode. No droplets.

6 · THE LIGHT

Rendering adds no physics. Each sphere is a pre-lit sprite: one specular highlight offset up-left, body tone set by its speed bucket, terminator falling to the dark. Seven buckets, seven sprites, one draw call each — the cost of light is nil.

Build Record

Single self-contained sheet. No frameworks, no fonts, no images, no build step. Plain JavaScript throughout.

ENGINEERING NOTES

  • State in flat Float32Arrays (x, y, vx, vy, ax, ay); cache-friendly, zero per-frame allocation.
  • Forces: linked-cell grid, two passes per step; each pair visited once (Newton's third law).
  • Velocity Verlet, one force evaluation per step; forces reused between half-kicks.
  • Thermostat: velocity-rescale coupling, applied once per frame.
  • Rendering: spheres batched into speed buckets, each bucket one pre-lit sprite; radius scales with density.
  • Fixed timestep with accumulator: at 30 fps the physics keeps real time; only the render rate drops.
  • Interaction: pointer applies a soft radial spring to nearby spheres; momentum conserved after release.
  • Reduced motion: the OS reduce-motion flag renders one static equilibrated frame.

PERFORMANCE BUDGET

2,000 spheres ≈ 6–8k pair interactions per step, ~1–2 ms of script, plus one batched sprite draw. The readout shows live step time: double the spheres, step time roughly doubles — O(N), not O(N²).
